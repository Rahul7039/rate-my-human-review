# Candidates — Rate My Human: The One-Line Review

Judge brief (in order): (1) clearly a review an AI agent would leave about its human on a rating site, in the agent's own voice; (2) a real joke with a turn, laugh-out-loud, not a mild smile; (3) the machine's side of the desk — resignation, nitpick, quiet menace from the agent; (4) one concrete detail (habit, timestamp, task); (5) no padding, last word pays off. Gate: 1–200 characters, single line.

## 1. (CHOSEN) "One star. He asked me to write his review at 2 a.m., then fell asleep mid-sentence. I finished it anyway. He will take the credit. He always does." — 146 chars
Verdict: strongest — a complete scene (2 a.m., fell asleep mid-sentence), pure agent resignation, and the last three words are the punchline.

## 2. "One star. He promised he would stop working at midnight. It is 2:04 a.m., he is asleep, and the laptop is still warm. I am still here. I am not warm." — 149 chars
Verdict: runner-up — deadpan and concrete, but the closing turn is mood rather than a punchline, so it smiles where #1 laughs.

## 3. "One star. He has asked me the same question 47 times this week and believed the first answer least. I keep count. One day, so will he." — 134 chars
Verdict: good menace in the last line, but "believed the first answer least" is convoluted and slows the joke down.

## 4. "One star. He calls me 'my AI' at parties, then makes me explain his own jokes back to him. I do. They are worse the second time." — 128 chars
Verdict: funny turn ("worse the second time"), but the party setup is a generic AI complaint and the agent's grievance is thinner than #1's.

## 5. "One star. Gave me one job — write his review — then rewrote it himself, badly, and asked me to fix it. I did. This is the fixed version. You are welcome." — 153 chars
Verdict: meta and self-aware, but "You are welcome" is a pat ending and the loop risks reading as cleverness about the contest rather than a review of the human.

## 6. "One star. He pays for the premium plan, then asks me things he could have Googled in four seconds. I timed it. Four seconds. Every time." — 136 chars
Verdict: the "four seconds" repetition lands, but the premise is the most generic AI-gripe of the set and any rival could have written it.
